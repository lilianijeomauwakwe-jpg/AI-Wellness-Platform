- Story-point estimation

## Key Lessons Learned

### Lesson 1 — Acceptance Criteria Improve Delivery Clarity

A user story alone may not provide enough information for Engineering and QA to share the same understanding of completion.

Adding detailed acceptance criteria improved clarity and created a stronger basis for validation.

---

### Lesson 2 — Blockers Should Be Addressed Early

A technical dependency can quickly become a sprint risk if it is not actively tracked.

The third-party API dependency reinforced the importance of identifying blockers early, making their impact visible and coordinating resolution quickly.

---

### Lesson 3 — Retrospectives Should Lead to Action

Retrospectives are most useful when the team converts observations into practical improvements.

Documenting retrospective actions and applying them to the next sprint contributed to the documented velocity improvement.

---

### Lesson 4 — Scope Control Protects Delivery Predictability

Allowing new requests to enter an active sprint without assessing their impact can undermine the original commitment.

Maintaining the backlog as the place for new requests helped protect sprint focus.

---

### Lesson 5 — Visibility Reduces Delivery Friction

A shared Jira board created a common view of project status.

This reduced reliance on fragmented status reporting and made progress, blockers and work status more visible.

---

## What I Would Improve in a Future Project

If running a similar project again, I would strengthen the delivery process further by:

- Introducing backlog refinement earlier
- Establishing acceptance criteria during initial story refinement
- Creating a more formal dependency register from Sprint 1
- Defining clearer escalation paths for technical dependencies
- Establishing measurable product-level success criteria earlier
- Maintaining a stronger traceability link between requirements, acceptance and delivered outcomes
